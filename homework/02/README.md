## Research Goal

I wanted to find out whether the listing process was understandable for someone without previous experience posting a car advertisement, and whether the form included the information they expected.

## Interview and Prototype Walkthrough

I spoke with my 18-year-old brother, who had never placed an online vehicle advertisement before. I showed him the Figma prototype and asked him to go through the listing screens.

He reached the end of the clickable flow without my help and understood the wording on the screens. He liked the simple interface and the fact that it did not feel overloaded with options.

He also noticed two important omissions:

- **Equipment:** There was no section for choosing the vehicle's features and extras.
- **Damage and repaired parts:** There was no structured place to describe body damage, paintwork or replaced panels.

This was an informal session with a family member, so his experience may differ from that of an experienced seller. The exact duration was not recorded. The Figma prototype was not a functional form, so the walkthrough did not involve entering real vehicle data.

## Main Findings

| Finding | What I learned | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A simple flow is useful for a first-time user | He understood the screens and could navigate independently | Keep the step-by-step layout |
| Equipment matters to listing completeness | He wanted to select a car's extras instead of only describing them in text | Add a structured equipment checklist |
| Vehicle history needs its own space | He expected a way to disclose damaged or replaced panels | Add a condition and repair-history section |

I grouped the notes into three themes in FigJam: **simplicity**, **equipment information**, and **condition transparency**. These themes come from one conversation and should be checked with more users.

This profile summarizes the participant I interviewed rather than inventing a separate research participant.

| --- | --- |
| Age | 18 |
| Previous listing experience | None |
| Goal | Understand how to create a detailed car listing |
| What worked | Clear screens and straightforward navigation |
| Main needs | Equipment choices and honest damage/repair details |
| Unknown | Actual car ownership, preferred device, real task duration |

## User Journey

| Stage | Experience or observation | Design opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | The purpose and main steps were understandable | Keep the introductory screen short |
| Vehicle details | Existing fields were understandable | Avoid unnecessary technical language |
| Equipment | He could not choose vehicle features | Introduce categories of extras |
| Condition and repairs | No place to mark damaged or replaced parts | Add body-panel statuses and repair notes |
| Photos, pricing and review | No specific complaint was recorded | Test actual data entry later |
| Completion | He reached the final Figma screen | Keep the confirmation clear |

## Revision and Follow-Up

I added an **Equipment & Condition** screen to the main Figma flow. When I showed him the first revision, he liked it but said the equipment list could be developed further. I then expanded the sample from four items to **16 features grouped into Safety & Driver Assistance, Comfort & Interior, Infotainment & Connectivity, and Exterior & Parking**.
