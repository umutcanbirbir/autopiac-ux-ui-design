# AutoPiac — User Interview R01

**Research notes:** 21 November 2026  
**Participant:** R01, age 18  
**Method:** Informal conversation and Figma walkthrough  
**Prototype:** [AutoPiac](https://www.figma.com/design/Po5hpum1mNJ7VnfVc0FTmC)

## Why I Did This Interview

I wanted to see whether a first-time user could follow the car-listing process and whether any essential information was missing.

R01 is my brother. He is 18 and has never published a car advertisement online. Because he is a family member and has no prior selling experience, his feedback is useful as an initial perspective but cannot represent every potential seller.

## What Happened

I showed him the clickable Figma screens and asked him to go through the listing process. He understood the interface and followed the available navigation without assistance. He said he liked the simplicity and the limited number of choices.

He pointed out two missing areas:

- **Equipment:** There was no way to choose the car's features, such as navigation or parking equipment.
- **Damage / replacement history:** There was no dedicated section for damaged, repainted or replaced body panels.

No other specific problems were mentioned during this conversation. I did not record an exact session duration, and the prototype did not support real data entry.

## Key Findings

| Finding | Participant feedback | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The main steps were easy to follow | He understood the screens and navigated independently | Keep the guided step-by-step flow |
| The equipment list was missing | He wanted to specify which features the vehicle has | Add grouped equipment options |
| The vehicle history section was missing | He wanted a way to describe damage and replaced parts | Add clear condition statuses and repair notes |

## Affinity Themes

I grouped these notes into three themes on the [FigJam discovery board](https://www.figma.com/board/FReaY560jfsj9gfCeta8oB):

1. **Simple navigation:** Keep the process straightforward.
2. **Complete vehicle information:** Give equipment its own section.
3. **Condition transparency:** Let sellers describe body condition and repairs.

## Persona Snapshot

**First-Time Prospective Seller**

- **Age:** 18
- **Previous listing experience:** None
- **What worked:** Straightforward navigation and understandable labels
- **What was missing:** Equipment choices and a way to report damage or replaced parts
- **Needs to be checked with others:** Whether more experienced sellers expect additional technical details

This snapshot describes R01's feedback. It is not intended to represent a large group of users.

## Journey and Design Opportunities

| Step | What I found | Change considered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start listing | Navigation was understandable | Keep the introduction simple |
| Vehicle details | The basic step was understandable | Add equipment and condition information |
| Equipment | No dedicated choices | Group extras by category |
| Condition | Damage and replaced parts were not covered | Add body-panel statuses and repair notes |
| Photos and price | No specific complaint was recorded | Test these steps with more participants |
| Review | He could navigate to the end | Show equipment and condition in the summary |

## What Changed

Based on these comments, I added an [Equipment & Condition screen](https://www.figma.com/design/Po5hpum1mNJ7VnfVc0FTmC?node-id=2007-2) and connected it to the listing flow. It shows sample feature choices and panel statuses such as Original, Repainted, Replaced, Damaged and Unknown.

During a follow-up conversation, R01 liked the revised screen but felt the equipment list still needed more options. The next design uses four groups and 16 features. [Follow-up notes](r01-follow-up-2026-11-21.md).

The newer version should be tested with other users. Figma's selections remain illustrative and do not store entered values.
